## Review Focus

1. **Zero, negative and constant columns** in methods that divide, take ratios or raise to powers (CODAS, MARCOS, WISP and COPRAS cost sums, CoCoSo `min S`/`min P`, CRITIC σ = 0):
   - A user expects a `ValueError` that names the method and the column. Where the scores are fuzzy, the message gives the remedy (`score="normalized"`).
   - A constant column must be neutral wherever the method allows it.
   - A `ZeroDivisionError`, NaN or silent garbage is a defect.
   - Tests: Tasks 4–7, `test_<method>_degenerate_inputs`.
2. **Negative default scores flowing into ratio methods.** Orthopair and IV defaults are signed differences in [−1, 1], so `rank_fuzzy(..., method="marcos")` and `ahp_fuzzy_numbers` with IVIFN input would see negatives.
   - A user expects a clear `ValueError` suggesting `score="normalized"`, never a sign-flipped ranking.
   - Tests: Task 8 `test_negative_scores_are_rejected_with_hint` and Task 10 `test_ratio_methods_reject_signed_scores`.
3. **Interval bounds that invert:**
   - On input: `mu_l > mu_u` or `t_l > t_u` in IV numbers, or a lower IT2 trapezoid poking out of the upper one.
   - After arithmetic: the upper pair projected onto the boundary drops below the lower one, or an IT2 product leaves the FOU.
   - A user expects inversion beyond `TOL` to raise `ValueError` naming the fields, and inversion within `TOL` to be clamped silently.
   - Tests: Tasks 1–3, `test_bounds_inversion_*` and the hypothesis closure tests.
4. **DEMATEL with a singular `I − N`:**
   - Example: every row sum equals the normalising maximum, e.g. `[[0, 1], [1, 0]]`. Also an all-zero or negative matrix.
   - A user expects a `ValueError` that says why, not a `ZeroDivisionError` or a meaningless huge T.
   - Test: Task 9 `test_singular_and_invalid_matrices`.
5. **Ties and degenerate pairs in pairwise methods:**
   - ELECTRE between identical alternatives (discordance 0/0).
   - CODAS with `|E_i − E_k|` exactly at τ (ψ uses ≥).
   - CoCoSo when one alternative is worst on every criterion (`min S = 0`).
   - A user expects defined results (0, the documented ψ boundary) or a clear `ValueError`, and stable tie order.
   - Tests: Tasks 4–6, `test_<method>_ties_and_identical_alternatives`.

**Interfaces:**
- Consumes: `orthopair._BINARY`, `_UNARY`, `_overshoot_ulps`, `_EPS`, `_PROJECT_ULPS`, `_ROUNDING`; `_validate.TOL`, `choose`, `finite`, `unit`; `FuzzyNumber`.
- Produces:
  - `IVQROFN(mu_l: float, mu_u: float, nu_l: float, nu_u: float, q: int)`, a frozen dataclass and `FuzzyNumber`.
  - Fixed-q subclasses `IVIFN`, `IVPFN` and `IVFFN` (q = 1, 2, 3). They are constructed without `q`, like `_Fixed`, and accept `q=` only for `dataclasses.replace`.
  - Class attributes: `OPS = ("algebraic", "einstein")`, `SCORES = ("difference", "normalized")`, `DEFAULT_SCORE = "difference"`, `ACCURACIES = ("sum",)`, `DISTANCES = ("hamming", "euclidean")`, `DEFAULT_DISTANCE = "hamming"`.
  - Methods: `add`, `mul`, `scale`, `power`, `score`, `accuracy`, `distance` and `complement()`.
  - Property `hesitancy -> tuple[float, float]`.
  - Classmethod `from_qrofn(x: QROFN) -> IVQROFN`, which builds the degenerate interval. On fixed subclasses it requires the same rung.
  - repr: `IVIFN(mu_l=0.4, mu_u=0.5, nu_l=0.3, nu_u=0.4)`.
- **Rulings:**
  - **Validation:**
    - Each bound must lie in [0, 1].
    - `mu_l <= mu_u` and `nu_l <= nu_u`. An inversion of up to `TOL` is clamped (lower := upper). Beyond `TOL` it raises `ValueError("need mu_l <= mu_u, got mu_l=..., mu_u=...")`.
    - The constraint `mu_u^q + nu_u^q <= 1` is checked and projected exactly as `QROFN` does on `(mu, nu)`. After projection the lower bounds are clamped to `min(lower, upper)`.
  - **Operations:**
    - With $a = \mu^q$ and $b = \nu^q$, apply the v0.1 law to the lower pair $(a^L, b^L)$ and to the upper pair $(a^U, b^U)$ separately. The result is $\mu$ = [first part from the lower pair, first part from the upper pair], and likewise for $\nu$.
    - Each v0.1 law is monotone in every argument, so lower ≤ upper is preserved. The upper pair carries the constraint.
    - Use the same slack as `QROFN._unary`.
    - For q = 1 algebraic this is Xu (2007) IIFWA arithmetic:
      $\tilde a \oplus \tilde b = ([a^L + b^L - a^L b^L,\, a^U + b^U - a^U b^U], [c^L d^L,\, c^U d^U])$.
  - **Scores:**
    - `"difference"`: $S = \tfrac12\left(\mu_L^q + \mu_U^q - \nu_L^q - \nu_U^q\right)$.
    - `"normalized"`: $(1 + S)/2$.
    - Accuracy `"sum"`: $H = \tfrac12\left(\mu_L^q + \mu_U^q + \nu_L^q + \nu_U^q\right)$.
    - For q = 1 these are Xu (2007). Verify, and if Peng & Yang (2016), Garg (2016) or Jeevaraj (2021) define a different IVPFN or IVFFN score, add it as an extra named score; never change these defaults.
  - **Distances:**
    - Work on $(a^L, a^U, b^L, b^U, \pi^L, \pi^U)$ with $\pi^L = 1 - a^U - b^U$ and $\pi^U = 1 - a^L - b^L$.
    - Hamming $= \tfrac14 \sum |\Delta|$. Euclidean $= \sqrt{\tfrac14 \sum \Delta^2}$.
    - For degenerate intervals these reduce to the v0.1 QROFN distances.
  - **Hesitancy:** $((1 - a^U - b^U)^{1/q},\, (1 - a^L - b^L)^{1/q})$.
  - **Complement:** $([\nu_L, \nu_U], [\mu_L, \mu_U])$.
- **Sources (verify):**
  - Atanassov & Gargov (1989), *Fuzzy Sets and Systems* 31(3), 343–349 (IVIFS).
  - Xu (2007), *Control and Decision* 22(2), 215–219 (IIFWA/IIFWG, score and accuracy).
  - Wang & Liu (2013), *Computers & Mathematics with Applications* 66(10), 1845–1856 (IVIF Einstein; verify that this is the right paper for the Einstein laws).
  - Peng & Yang (2016), *International Journal of Intelligent Systems* 31(5), 444–487 (IVPFN).
  - Garg (2016), *Journal of Intelligent & Fuzzy Systems* 31(1), 529–540 (IVPFN accuracy).
  - Jeevaraj (2021), *Expert Systems with Applications* 185, 115613 (IVFFN).
  - Joshi, Singh, Bhatt & Vaisla (2018), *Journal of Intelligent & Fuzzy Systems* 35(5), 5225–5230 (IVq-ROFS).

- Modify: the exports in both `__init__.py`, `weighting._ALL_SCORES` and `tests/strategies.py` (`ivneutrosophics()`).

**Interfaces:**
- Consumes: `threepart._one_minus_pow`, `_pow`; `orthopair` helpers as needed; `FuzzyNumber`.
- Produces:
  - `IVNeutrosophicFN(t_l, t_u, i_l, i_u, f_l, f_u)` with `OPS = ("algebraic",)`.
  - Scores: `SCORES = ("average",)`. Accuracies: `ACCURACIES = ("difference", "certainty")`, default `"difference"`. Distances: `DISTANCES = ("hamming", "euclidean")`.
  - Methods: `complement()` and classmethod `from_neutrosophic(x: NeutrosophicFN)`.
- **Rulings:**
  - **Validation:** each bound is in [0, 1], each `*_l <= *_u` (TOL clamp as in Task 1), and there is no joint constraint (Wang et al. 2005).
  - **Operations:** the v0.1 `NeutrosophicFN` laws applied to the lower and upper bounds separately. This is the arithmetic of Zhang, Wang & Chen (2014):
    - add: $T = [t^L_1 + t^L_2 - t^L_1 t^L_2, \dots]$, $I = [i^L_1 i^L_2, i^U_1 i^U_2]$, $F = [f^L_1 f^L_2, f^U_1 f^U_2]$;
    - mul: the dual;
    - scale and power: per bound.
  - **Score:** `"average"` $= \tfrac16\left(4 + t_L + t_U - i_L - i_U - f_L - f_U\right)$, the v0.1 average score on the interval midpoints.
    - If the verified source (Zhang et al. 2014, or the score paper it cites, e.g. Şahin 2015) defines another score, add that one under its own name. Keep `"average"` as the default for continuity with `NeutrosophicFN`.
  - **Accuracy:** `"difference"` $= \tfrac12(t_L + t_U - f_L - f_U)$ and `"certainty"` $= \tfrac12(t_L + t_U)$.
  - **Distances:** over the six bounds, divided by 6 (Hamming) or under a square root of the sum divided by 6 (Euclidean). For degenerate intervals they reduce to v0.1.
  - **Complement:** $([f_L, f_U], [1 - i_U, 1 - i_L], [t_L, t_U])$ (Wang et al. 2005).
- **Sources (verify):**
  - Wang, Smarandache, Zhang & Sunderraman (2005), *Interval Neutrosophic Sets and Logic: Theory and Applications in Computing*, Hexis (no DOI expected).
  - Zhang, Wang & Chen (2014), *The Scientific World Journal* 2014, 645953 (operations, INNWAA/INNWGA).

### Task 3: Interval type-2 trapezoidal fuzzy numbers (IT2TrFN)

**Files:**
- Create: `src/bulanik/numbers/it2.py`, `tests/test_it2_number.py`
- Modify: the exports, `weighting._ALL_SCORES` and `tests/strategies.py` (`it2trfns(positive=False)`).

**Interfaces:**
- Consumes: `TrFN`, `TFN`, `FuzzyNumber`, `_validate`.
- Produces:
  - `IT2TrFN(upper: Sequence[float], lower: Sequence[float])`, a frozen dataclass storing two 6-tuples of floats, `(a1, a2, a3, a4, h1, h2)` each. `h1` is the membership at `a2` and `h2` the membership at `a3`.
  - Attributes: `OPS = ("algebraic",)`, `SCORES = ("dtrat", "centroid", "chen_lee")`, `DEFAULT_SCORE = "dtrat"`, `ACCURACIES = ()`, `DISTANCES = ("vertex",)`.
  - Methods: `add`, `sub`, `mul`, `scale`, `power` and `__sub__`.
  - `accuracy()` raises `ValueError("IT2TrFN has no accuracy function")`, as `TFN` does.
  - Classmethod `from_trfn(x: TrFN | TFN) -> IT2TrFN`, which sets `upper = lower = (points, 1, 1)`.
- **Rulings:**
  - **Validation:**
    - Points are finite and ordered within each trapezoid.
    - Heights are in [0, 1], and the upper trapezoid needs `max(h1, h2) > 0`.
    - The lower membership must be ≤ the upper membership (+TOL) at every breakpoint of either trapezoid. Evaluate both memberships with the closed-graph maximum at vertical edges. This is sufficient, because both functions are linear between the merged breakpoints.
    - On violation, raise `ValueError` naming the point.
  - **Arithmetic (Chen & Lee 2010):** apply each rule to the upper and to the lower trapezoid. Heights combine with `min`.
    - add: points add.
    - sub: $(a_1 - b_4, a_2 - b_3, a_3 - b_2, a_4 - b_1)$.
    - mul: componentwise products. All points must be ≥ 0, otherwise `ValueError`.
    - scale by $k$: points times $k$, heights unchanged. For $k < 0$ the points reverse and `h1`/`h2` swap.
    - power $k$: points to the $k$, heights unchanged. The lowest point must be ≥ 0 (> 0 when $k < 0$, which reverses the points and swaps the heights).
    - The power and mul rules give Kahraman et al.'s (2014) geometric mean $(\prod \tilde a)^{1/n}$.
    - Results go through full validation (Review Focus 3).
  - **`"dtrat"` (Kahraman et al. 2014):**
    $$\mathrm{DTraT} = \tfrac12\left[\frac{(a^U_4 - a^U_1) + (h^U_1 a^U_2 - a^U_1) + (h^U_2 a^U_3 - a^U_1)}{4} + a^U_1 + \frac{(a^L_4 - a^L_1) + (h^L_1 a^L_2 - a^L_1) + (h^L_2 a^L_3 - a^L_1)}{4} + a^L_1\right]$$
    For `from_trfn` this reduces to the TrFN mean.
  - **`"centroid"`:** the mean of the x-centroids of the upper and lower polygons $(a_1, 0), (a_2, h_1), (a_3, h_2), (a_4, 0)$, by the shoelace formula. A zero-area polygon uses the mean of its points. This is bulanik's own choice, not the Karnik–Mendel centroid; document it.
  - **`"chen_lee"`** (the ranking value of Chen & Lee 2010, *moderately confident*; verify every term, especially the 1/4 factor and population vs sample standard deviation, and pin the paper's worked ranking values):
    $$\mathrm{Rank} = \sum_{X \in \{U, L\}} \Big[\sum_{p=1}^{3} M_p(X) - \tfrac14 \sum_{q=1}^{4} S_q(X) + h_1(X) + h_2(X)\Big]$$
    with $M_p = (a_p + a_{p+1})/2$, $S_q$ the standard deviation of $\{a_q, a_{q+1}\}$ for $q \le 3$, and $S_4$ the standard deviation of $\{a_1, \dots, a_4\}$.
  - **`"vertex"` distance:** $\sqrt{\tfrac18 \sum (\Delta a)^2}$ over the 8 points. Heights are ignored (documented). For `from_trfn` this equals the TrFN vertex distance.
- **Sources (verify):**
  - Chen & Lee (2010a), *Expert Systems with Applications* 37(1), 824–833 (arithmetic, ranking value).
  - Chen & Lee (2010b), *Expert Systems with Applications* 37(4), 2790–2798 (IT2 TOPSIS, linguistic scale).
  - Kahraman, Öztayşi, Uçal Sarı & Turanoğlu (2014), *Knowledge-Based Systems* 59, 48–57 (DTraT, IT2 AHP).
  - Mendel, John & Liu (2006), *IEEE Transactions on Fuzzy Systems* 14(6), 808–821 (terminology).

- **CODAS** (Keshavarz Ghorabaee, Zavadskas, Turskis & Antucheviciene 2016, *Economic Computation and Economic Cybernetics Studies and Research* 50(3), 25–44; verify):
  - $n_{ij} = x_{ij}/\max_i x_{ij}$ (benefit), $\min_i x_{ij}/x_{ij}$ (cost); $r_{ij} = w_j n_{ij}$; $ns_j = \min_i r_{ij}$.
  - $E_i = \sqrt{\sum_j (r_{ij} - ns_j)^2}$ and $T_i = \sum_j |r_{ij} - ns_j|$.
  - $h_{ik} = (E_i - E_k) + \psi(E_i - E_k)(T_i - T_k)$ with $\psi(x) = 1$ if $|x| \ge \tau$, else 0.
  - $H_i = \sum_k h_{ik}$, larger is better.
  - `extras`: `E`, `T`. `operators = {"normalization": "linear_max", "tau": str(tau)}`.
  - Entries must be > 0 (ValueError with the WASPAS-style message and score hint). τ must be in [0, 1].
- **COPRAS** (Zavadskas & Kaklauskas 1996, in Langford & Retik (eds.), *The Organization and Management of Construction*, Vol. 2, 94–104; also Zavadskas, Kaklauskas & Šarka 1994, *Technological and Economic Development of Economy* 1(3), 131–139; verify):
  - $d_{ij} = w_j x_{ij} / \sum_i x_{ij}$; $S^+_i = \sum_{\text{benefit}} d_{ij}$ and $S^-_i = \sum_{\text{cost}} d_{ij}$.
  - $Q_i = S^+_i + \dfrac{\sum_k S^-_k}{S^-_i \sum_k 1/S^-_k}$ (just $S^+_i$ when there are no cost criteria).
  - `scores` = $Q$. `extras`: `S_plus`, `S_minus` and `utility` $= 100\,Q_i/\max Q$. `operators = {"normalization": "sum"}`.
  - **Ruling:** entries must be ≥ 0 and every column sum > 0. When a cost criterion exists, every $S^-_i$ must be > 0; otherwise raise `ValueError` naming the alternative.

- **MARCOS** (Stević, Pamučar, Puška & Chatterjee 2020, *Computers & Industrial Engineering* 140, 106231; verify):
  - Extend the matrix with the anti-ideal AAI (min for benefit, max for cost) and the ideal AI (max for benefit, min for cost).
  - $n_{ij} = x_{ij}/x_{AI,j}$ (benefit) and $x_{AI,j}/x_{ij}$ (cost); $v_{ij} = w_j n_{ij}$; $S_i = \sum_j v_{ij}$, including the AAI and AI rows.
  - $K^-_i = S_i/S_{AAI}$, $K^+_i = S_i/S_{AI}$; $f(K^-_i) = K^+_i/(K^+_i + K^-_i)$, $f(K^+_i) = K^-_i/(K^+_i + K^-_i)$.
  - $f(K_i) = \dfrac{K^+_i + K^-_i}{1 + \frac{1 - f(K^+_i)}{f(K^+_i)} + \frac{1 - f(K^-_i)}{f(K^-_i)}}$, larger is better.
  - `extras`: `K_minus`, `K_plus` and `S`. `operators = {"normalization": "ideal_ratio"}`. Entries must be > 0.
- **CoCoSo** (Yazdani, Zarate, Zavadskas & Turskis 2019, *Management Decision* 57(9), 2501–2519; verify):
  - $r_{ij} = (x_{ij} - \min_i x_{ij})/(\max_i - \min_i)$ (benefit), $(\max_i - x_{ij})/(\max_i - \min_i)$ (cost).
  - $S_i = \sum_j w_j r_{ij}$ and $P_i = \sum_{j: w_j > 0} r_{ij}^{\,w_j}$.
  - $k_{ia} = (P_i + S_i)/\sum_i (P_i + S_i)$, $k_{ib} = S_i/\min S + P_i/\min P$ and $k_{ic} = (\lambda S_i + (1-\lambda) P_i)/(\lambda \max S + (1-\lambda) \max P)$.
  - $k_i = (k_{ia} k_{ib} k_{ic})^{1/3} + (k_{ia} + k_{ib} + k_{ic})/3$.
  - `extras`: `S`, `P`, `k_a`, `k_b` and `k_c`. `operators = {"normalization": "min_max", "lambda": str(lam)}`.
  - **Rulings:**
    - A constant column gets $r_{ij} = 0$ for every alternative, which is neutral for both S and P.
    - `min S == 0` or `min P == 0` (an alternative worst on every weighted criterion) raises `ValueError("CoCoSo: alternative 'A1' is worst on every criterion, so k_b divides by zero")`.
    - Any finite entries are allowed.
- **WISP** (Stanujkić, Popović, Karabašević, Meidutė-Kavaliauskienė & Ulutaş, *IEEE Transactions on Engineering Management*, published online 2021 with DOI 10.1109/TEM.2021.3075783, issue year to verify):
  - $r_{ij} = x_{ij}/\max_i x_{ij}$ for every criterion.
  - With $\Omega_{\max}$ the benefit criteria and $\Omega_{\min}$ the cost criteria:
    $u^{sd}_i = \sum_{\max} w r - \sum_{\min} w r$, $u^{pd}_i = \prod_{\max} r^w - \prod_{\min} r^w$, $u^{sr}_i = \sum_{\max} w r / \sum_{\min} w r$ and $u^{pr}_i = \prod_{\max} r^w / \prod_{\min} r^w$.
  - Each measure is recalculated as $\bar u_i = (1 + u_i)/(1 + \max_k u_k)$, and $u_i = \tfrac14(\bar u^{sd} + \bar u^{pd} + \bar u^{sr} + \bar u^{pr})$.
  - `extras`: the four `u_*` measures. `operators = {"normalization": "linear_max"}`. Entries must be > 0.
  - **Ruling:** with an empty $\Omega_{\min}$ or $\Omega_{\max}$, implement what the paper prescribes. If the paper is silent, use these conventions and document them:
    - an empty sum is 0 and an empty product is 1;
    - the ratio forms then use denominator 1, or a numerator of 0 (sum) or 1 (product).

- **CRITIC** (Diakoulaki, Mavrotas & Papayannakis 1995, *Computers & Operations Research* 22(7), 763–770; verify):

  - Criteria come in any order. $s_j$ for $j \ge 2$ compares criterion $j$ with $j-1$.
  - $k_1 = 1$, $k_j = 2 - s_j$; $q_1 = 1$, $q_j = q_{j-1}/k_j$; $w_j = q_j/\sum q$.

    - Crisp inputs use the formula above, and every $k_j$ must be > 0, i.e. $s_j < 2$; otherwise `ValueError`.

```python
    total: tuple[tuple[float, ...], ...]           # T = N (I - N)^-1
    d: dict[str, float]; r: dict[str, float]       # row / column sums of T
    prominence: dict[str, float]; relation: dict[str, float]   # D + R, D - R
    causes: tuple[str, ...]; effects: tuple[str, ...]          # relation > 0 / <= 0
    threshold: float
    relations: tuple[tuple[str, str, float], ...]  # (from, to, t_ij) with t_ij > threshold, i != j
    weights: dict[str, float]; ranking: tuple[str, ...]
    operators: dict[str, str]
    extras: dict[str, Any] = field(default_factory=dict)
    def as_weights(self) -> WeightingResult: ...   # method "dematel"; usable as the weights of every ranking method
```

- **Method** (Gabus & Fontela 1972, *World Problems, an Invitation to Further Thought within the Framework of DEMATEL*, Battelle Geneva Research Centre; CFCS: Opricovic & Tzeng 2003, *International Journal of Uncertainty, Fuzziness and Knowledge-Based Systems* 11(5), 635–652; fuzzy DEMATEL with CFCS: Wu & Lee 2007, *Expert Systems with Applications* 32(2), 499–507; verify):
  - $N = A/s$, with $s = \max(\max_i \sum_j a_{ij},\, \max_j \sum_i a_{ij})$ for `"max_row_col"` or $s = \max_i \sum_j a_{ij}$ for `"max_row"`.
  - $T = N(I - N)^{-1}$. $D$ holds the row sums of $T$ and $R$ the column sums.
  - The threshold defaults to the mean of all $n^2$ entries of $T$.
  - Weights:
    - `"sqrt"`: $w_i \propto \sqrt{(D_i+R_i)^2 + (D_i-R_i)^2}$;
    - `"prominence"`: $w_i \propto D_i + R_i$.
    - Cite a verified source for the sqrt form. If none can be verified, document it as a common convention.
  - CFCS for a TFN $(l, m, r)$ with $\min = \min l$, $\max = \max r$ and $\Delta = \max - \min$:
    - $x_l = (l - \min)/\Delta$, $x_m$ and $x_r$ likewise;
    - $x^{ls} = x_m/(1 + x_m - x_l)$ and $x^{rs} = x_r/(1 + x_r - x_m)$;
    - $x = [x^{ls}(1 - x^{ls}) + x^{rs} x^{rs}]/(1 - x^{ls} + x^{rs})$;
    - $z = \min + x\Delta$, averaged over the experts.
    - **Ruling:** take the scope of min and max (whole matrix vs per cell across experts) from Opricovic & Tzeng (2003), reproduce Wu & Lee's (2007) numbers, and document the scope.
    - If $\Delta = 0$, $z = \min$.
- **Rulings:**
  - Crisp entries must be finite and ≥ 0. The diagonal is used as given. $n \ge 2$.
  - An all-zero matrix ($s = 0$) raises `ValueError`. A singular $I - N$ raises `ValueError` explaining that the normalised matrix has spectral radius 1 (e.g. equal row and column sums).
  - Fuzzy entries must all be one kind.
  - `defuzzification=None` means `"cfcs"` for TFN and the kind's `DEFAULT_SCORE` otherwise. Any score name of the kind is accepted, and `"cfcs"` only for TFN. Defuzzified negatives raise the crisp error with a `score="normalized"` hint.
  - Factor names default to `F1..Fn`.
  - `operators = {"normalization": ..., "defuzzification": "crisp" | name, "threshold": "mean" | str(value), "weights": weighting}`.
